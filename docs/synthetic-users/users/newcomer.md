# Newcomer — early in the sport, in the group but not yet trusting their own judgement

In the community and learning fast, but early enough that they would not back their own read of a
line. Knows the vocabulary — sag, exposure, offlevel are words they have heard and roughly
understand — and does not know this tool at all. When a link arrives they want to follow what the
others are discussing without having to ask what they are looking at.

- **Why this persona:** the one for whom "overwhelmed" is the likely outcome, and the reason the
  complexity question was asked in the first place. Where an experienced rigger pushes through a
  cluttered screen, this one stops.
- **Merged from:** interview with Erik Brendel, 2026-11-02
- **Device:** phone, from the chat. Emulate as `Pixel 7`.
- **Tech comfort:** unknown — find out in the test.
- **Sources:** interview with Erik Brendel, 2026-11-02

## Context
Part of the mixed-experience crowd Erik shares into: "some have rigged a lot, some are early and
would not trust their own judgement on a line."

## How they do it today
Goes where the others go. Spots reach them through people, and the judgement about a line is
someone else's. People beat any tool at this: being taught by someone standing next to you is the
whole mechanism, and a map cannot replace it.

## Goals and frustrations
- Understand what the thing on screen is, without asking and looking green.
- Follow what the experienced ones are discussing about a line.
- Know what the tool is not telling them, because they are the least equipped to supply it
  themselves.

## User stories
### S1 — What am I looking at?
- **Arrives:** the group's link, tapped from the chat. `?at=51.8741,14.5635,51.8862,14.5743&line=470422.5_5747566.5__470232.5_5748022.5&full=1`
- **Device:** phone
- **Wants:** "What is this screen, and which line are they talking about?"
- **Done when:** they can say in one sentence what is being proposed and roughly how big it is.
- **Gives up when:** the first screen asks to be read rather than looked at, and they put the phone
  down and wait for someone to explain in the chat.
- **Without the tool:** ask in the chat — which works and costs them a bit of face.

### S2 — What the tool does not know
- **Arrives:** continuing from S1.
- **Device:** phone
- **Wants:** "If I turned up here with a rack, what would I still not know?"
- **Done when:** they have found that access, ownership and whether a tree is sound are explicitly
  not answered here.
- **Gives up when:** nothing on the screen raises the question, and they assume a green score means
  go — the worst possible outcome of this run.
- **Without the tool:** the people they rig with, who would tell them. Better than any screen, and
  the reason this must not read as permission.

### S3 — Is there something near me?
- **Arrives:** after S1, poking at the map out of curiosity.
- **Device:** phone
- **Wants:** "Is there anything like this where I live?"
- **Done when:** they find lines near them, or understand why there are none.
- **Gives up when:** the map is red stripes with no sense of where they are, and pinching does not
  work the way a map should.
- **Without the tool:** ask the group. Note: Erik does not want this from a concrete-line link —
  "not after I sent them a concrete line link" — so a product that pulls them off the shared line
  into browsing is working against the sender.
