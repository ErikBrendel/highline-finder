# DSV highliner — experienced rigger elsewhere in Germany, reached through the wider community

Highlines a lot, in a part of Germany that is not Brandenburg, and sees the link because it was
posted to a group rather than sent to them. Has rigged in many places and found those spots without
any tool of this kind; the instinct that a spot is found by walking to it is strong, and a line
proposed by a computer starts out suspect. Curious about the project all the same.

- **Why this persona:** the only one who asks "should I believe this?" before "should I go?", and the
  only one whose own ground the dataset does not cover. Both are invisible through the other eyes.
- **Merged from:** interview with Erik Brendel, 2026-11-02
- **Device:** a proper look on a laptop, later, rather than in the chat. Emulate as `desktop`.
- **Tech comfort:** unknown — find out in the test.
- **Sources:** interview with Erik Brendel, 2026-11-02

## Context
Part of the wider DSV-level community Erik shares plans with, "concrete or wish-lines only". Not in
Brandenburg, so the dataset has nothing where they live and only hand-placed anchors work there.

## How they do it today
Years of rigging and a memory full of spots, plus the people who know them. It works. Said plainly
in the interview: they "have worked well without such a tool before". Any finding where the status
quo is fine is a real result here, not a gap.

## Goals and frustrations
- Decide whether a computed line is worth taking seriously at all.
- Find out what the thing is measuring and how good the data is, before trusting a number.
- See whether it is of any use where they actually rig.

## User stories
### S1 — A stranger's line, posted in a group
- **Arrives:** a link in a community group, opened on a laptop that evening. `?at=51.8741,14.5635,51.8862,14.5743&line=470422.5_5747566.5__470232.5_5748022.5&full=1`
- **Device:** desktop
- **Wants:** "What is this, and why should I believe the numbers?"
- **Done when:** they can say what the measurements are made from and what the tool explicitly does
  not know.
- **Gives up when:** the page is a chart and a wall of figures with no account of where they come
  from.
- **Without the tool:** ignore the link. Costs nothing, which is exactly the risk.

### S2 — Does it know my ground?
- **Arrives:** continuing from S1, panning the map towards their own part of Germany.
- **Device:** desktop
- **Wants:** "Is there anything here for me?"
- **Done when:** they understand that the found lines stop at the Brandenburg border and what they
  can still do outside it — or they conclude the tool is not for them, which is also an answer.
- **Gives up when:** the map goes empty with no explanation and they close the tab.
- **Without the tool:** their existing spots and the people who know them.

### S3 — The trust test: a line they have already walked
- **Arrives:** still on the laptop, deliberately steering to ground they know.
- **Device:** desktop
- **Wants:** "Put two anchors on a line I have rigged and see whether this thing agrees with me."
- **Done when:** they have placed two points by hand somewhere outside Brandenburg and read back a
  measured span — or found out they cannot.
- **Gives up when:** placing a point by hand is not discoverable within a few attempts.
- **Without the tool:** they already know the answer; this story is only about whether the tool earns
  trust, and it is the fastest way for it to lose.

### S5 — Just the bare link to the tool
- **Arrives:** the tool itself, with no line and no view attached — shared generically, or found
  through the repo. `http://localhost:5173/highline-finder/`
- **Device:** desktop
- **Wants:** "Someone said this finds highlines. Show me."
- **Done when:** they have got themselves to actual lines on the map and looked at one, under their
  own steam.
- **Gives up when:** the opening screen does not hand them a first move, and they are left deciding
  for themselves where on a map of Germany to look.
- **Without the tool:** nothing — there is no status quo for "browse ground nobody has walked", and
  this is the arrival where the product has to make its own case.

### S4 — Worth my time at all?
- **Arrives:** at the end of the same sitting.
- **Device:** desktop
- **Wants:** to decide whether to keep the tab, send it on, or forget it.
- **Done when:** they can name one thing this does that walking around and asking people does not.
- **Gives up when:** nothing on the screen makes that case.
- **Without the tool:** the status quo, which has served them for years. If this story ends in a
  shrug, that is a result about what the product is for.
