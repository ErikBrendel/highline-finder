# Rigging partner — highliner Erik rigs with, who gets the concrete line links

One of the people Erik actually rigs with. Experienced: sag, exposure and offlevel are everyday
words, and they have found spots the usual way for years — walking around, looking up, asking
people — without any tool like this. A link from Erik is a proposal, and their job is to decide
whether it is worth going. They know that whatever the link says, the real work is still ahead:
scouting it in person, and the access and land-owner conversations after that.

- **Why this persona:** the recipient the shared link was built for. If the full-screen view does not
  let an experienced highliner reach a yes-or-no in under a minute, nothing else about it matters.
- **Merged from:** interview with Erik Brendel, 2026-11-02
- **Device:** phone, tapped out of a chat app, often in that app's in-app browser; sometimes a proper
  look on a laptop later. Emulate as `iPhone 15`.
- **Tech comfort:** unknown — find out in the test. The one thing said about it: "humans have not
  much capacity to learn a complex new tool quickly."
- **Sources:** interview with Erik Brendel, 2026-11-02

## Context
Rigs with Erik. Gets links in a chat, where the message around the link carries the intent — whether
this is a concrete plan or a wish-line. Said of the group as a whole: very experienced with rigging
and with different spots, and they have worked well without such a tool before.

## How they do it today
Word of mouth, people who have been there, and walking the ground. The status quo is not broken:
this group found its spots without a map of computed candidates and would keep finding them. What
the tool can add is overview and reach — ground nobody has walked yet — not judgement.

The hand-off after a yes is **outside this tool entirely**: "a lot more planning happens outside of
this tool before a project actually starts. Usually this also means an actual in-world scouting
mission, and more legal / orga work with the people owning the land. The tool is just a first nice
impression and search-help." Expect nothing of the tool past that first impression.

## Goals and frustrations
- Reach a yes-or-no on a proposed line quickly, from a phone, in a chat.
- Judge whether a line is riggable at all: anchors, length, clearance.
- Not be made to learn a tool in order to answer a question about a line.
- Scepticism to overcome: a line a computer proposed has to earn trust that a line a friend walked to
  already has.

## User stories
### S1 — A line lands in the group chat
- **Arrives:** a link from Erik in a chat, opened by tapping it. `?at=51.8741,14.5635,51.8862,14.5743&line=470422.5_5747566.5__470232.5_5748022.5&full=1`
- **Device:** phone
- **Wants:** "Is this worth a Saturday?"
- **Done when:** they can say yes or no and name the one thing that decided it — the length, the
  height, the trees, the drive.
- **Gives up when:** three taps in they still cannot tell what they are looking at, and they go back
  to the chat to ask Erik instead.
- **Without the tool:** ask Erik in the chat — which works, and is one message. The tool only wins if
  looking is faster than asking.

### S2 — Second opinion before anyone packs gear
- **Arrives:** the same link, opened again on a laptop in the evening.
- **Device:** desktop
- **Wants:** "What are the anchors, how long is it really, and how much air is under it?"
- **Done when:** they have the length, the sag, the exposure and what the two ends actually are, and
  believe those numbers enough to repeat them to someone else.
- **Gives up when:** the numbers are there but nothing says where they come from or how sure they are.
- **Without the tool:** go and look. Still necessary — this only decides whether the trip happens.

### S3 — A wish-line, not a plan
- **Arrives:** a link posted with "wish-line" in the chat text. `?at=51.4776,14.0464,51.4879,14.0603&line=434469.5_5703802.5__434062.5_5704067.5&full=1`
- **Device:** phone
- **Wants:** "Could this ever work, or is it a dream?"
- **Done when:** they can tell how plausible the tool thinks it is — the score and clearance read as a
  verdict, not as decoration.
- **Gives up when:** the numbers do not separate a dream from a plan, and the line is just another
  red stripe.
- **Without the tool:** nothing. Nobody has walked this one, so there is no one to ask — this is the
  case the tool is actually for.

### S4 — From the link to the ground
- **Arrives:** having said yes in S1, still on the phone.
- **Device:** phone
- **Wants:** to get this spot into whatever they will use to actually drive there and walk in.
- **Done when:** they have a position they can hand to a maps app, or know the tool will not give
  them one. Unknown whether this is how it really goes — find out in the test.
- **Gives up when:** they cannot get a usable location out in two or three actions; they screenshot
  it and move on.
- **Without the tool:** ask for the coordinates in the chat.
